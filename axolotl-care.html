Before You Get One

Axolotls are not a starter pet. They need cold water year round, they cannot be handled, and they live ten to fifteen years. None of that makes them difficult exactly, but it does mean the decision is a long one.

What They Need

Cold Water

Keep the tank between 61 and 64 degrees Fahrenheit. Above 75 degrees an axolotl becomes stressed and can die, so most keepers need a chiller or a cool room rather than a heater. Chlorine in ordinary tap water is also harmful, so the water has to be treated with a conditioner first.

Room to Turn Around

A single adult typically needs about a 40 gallon tank. Axolotls grow to nine or ten inches and spend most of their time walking along the bottom, so floor space matters more than height. They also dislike strong currents, so a gentle filter works better than a powerful one.

No Gravel

Axolotls swallow whatever is under their food, and swallowed gravel causes fatal blockages. Use a bare bottom or fine sand with grains under one millimeter.

Worms, Not Flakes

Earthworms are the staple food. Salmon or trout pellets, frozen bloodworms, and waxworms also work. Feeder fish can carry parasites, so most keepers skip them.

One Last Thing

An axolotl has no scales and a mostly cartilage skeleton, so handling injures them easily. They are a watching pet, not a holding pet. Some places also restrict or prohibit keeping them, so check your state's rules before buying one.
